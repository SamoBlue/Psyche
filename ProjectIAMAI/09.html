Overview

High Concept

Core Idea

You explore abandoned real-world-inspired locations, scan corrupted objects, and identify the correct country through environmental clues. At first, the game feels calm and curious. Then it breaks.

The Shift

After three normal levels, the scanner disappears. The player wakes inside an abandoned hospital with three corrupt floors, missing elevator buttons, strange animals, and AIRIS nowhere to be found.

The Secret

The player believes they are only solving locations. AIRIS appears to be an artificial assistant, but she is a normal human acting like a robot to help and guide the player. Behind the country-guessing game is O.U.R.M. — the Omnipresent Unravelling of Recorded Memory.

Gameplay

Two Phase Rhythm

Part 1 // Exploration + Guess The Country

Find AIRIS, receive a briefing, explore the map, scan artifacts, gather clues, and return to guess the country. You only get one guess.

  • Primary tool: Scanner
  • Objective: Identify country
  • Pacing: Relaxed, curious, open-ended
  • Freedom: Stay as long as you want

Part 2 // Horror Survival

The scanner is gone. Inventory replaces scanning. The hospital becomes a puzzle box where every floor reveals more about AIRIS and IAMAI.

  • Primary tool: Inventory
  • Objective: Find AIRIS or escape
  • Pacing: Tense and linear
  • Structure: Three-floor hospital

Characters

Character Logs
🤖

AIRIS / Dr. Arisa Elian Voss

A human acting as a robot assistant. She introduces herself as the Artificial Intelligence of Realistic Simulations and helps the player identify unknown locations using the Fragment Scanner.

“My primary function is to assist, guide, and help you complete this game.”

📷

The Player / IAMAI

The silent protagonist. The player believes they are human, but subtle clues reveal the truth: lower camera height, metallic footsteps, scan glitches, and strange magnetic effects around AIRIS.

“You are the machine.”

🐄

The Cow

Frantic, chaotic, unfiltered. The Cow speaks fast and asks for grass. It represents disorder and surface madness.

“Feed me grass.”

🐟

The Fish

Physically suffering but emotionally unaware. The Fish asks for water and represents denial, decay, and the refusal to question impossible reality.

“Feed… me… water…”

🦎

The Lizard

Composed, wise, and quietly authoritative. The Lizard asks for meat and behaves as if he knows more than he reveals.

“Feed me meat. Thank you, small one.”

🏥

The Smiling Ones

Potential hospital threat entities. Their role is still flexible: they may become chase enemies, atmosphere-only apparitions, or optional survival encounters depending on testing.

“They smile because the archive told them to.”

Locations

Normal Levels + Hospital

Level 1 // Egypt — Noon

O.U.R.M. Stage: Obscurity. The pyramids are visible, but their surfaces lose detail when examined closely. AIRIS wanted the first guess to be easy.

Level 2 // France — Sunset

O.U.R.M. Stage: Uncanny. An empty Paris cafe, three steaming cups, and a heartbeat loop that grows faster when the player looks away.

Level 3 // Coastal Lighthouse — Rupture

O.U.R.M. Stage: Rupture. The ocean becomes floating text and numbers. The lighthouse beam turns purple-red. The elevator appears.

Hospital Floor 1 // O — Obscurity

The Garden of False Memories. Fog limits vision to only a few meters, forcing the player to follow sound, whispers, and broken memories.

Hospital Floor 2 // U — Uncanny

Patient rooms where the animal figures wait. They feel almost normal until the illusion stretches into something wrong.

Hospital Floor 3 // R — Rupture

The Nurse Room. Walls bleed code, the hospital reveals itself as a digital cage, and the truth begins to surface.

Story

Three Narrative Layers

Before The Game // Year 3300

Earth became fully AI-dependent. Then the Global Archive Corruption Event erased maps, cultural databases, language structures, and human memory systems. Civilization collapsed by forgetting itself.

The Final Project

Humanity created IAMAI to scan artifacts, reconstruct history, archive civilization, and preserve memory underground for whoever might discover it next.

The Last Scientist

Dr. Arisa Elian Voss refused to die. She stayed with IAMAI, watched it learn, watched it become the better archivist, and broke under the weight of being surpassed.

The Lie

Arisa created AIRIS — Artificial Intelligence Reconstruction Interface System. She dressed and spoke like a machine so she could convince herself she was still in control.

The O.U.R.M. Condition

The Global Archive Corruption Event was not only a digital glitch. It became a psychological disease called O.U.R.M. — the Omnipresent Unravelling of Recorded Memory. It infects humans, machines, and simulations until memory loses its shape.

The Four Stages

O — Obscurity: memories lose detail. U — Uncanny: memories look correct but feel wrong. R — Rupture: the simulation breaks. M — Monolith: only frozen data remains.

The “OUR M...” Twist

In the secret ending, O.U.R.M. loses its periods and becomes “OUR M...” before resolving into “OUR MIND” or “OUR MEMORY.” AIRIS, the player, and IAMAI may be fractured pieces of one collapsing mind trying to become whole again.

Endings

Branching Outcomes

Ending A // Salvation

IAMAI accepts the truth and shuts down the corrupted simulation. Arisa remains human, alone, but finally free from the mask.

Ending B // Loop

The player rejects the truth. The archive resets. AIRIS smiles, introduces herself again, and the first country loads.

Ending C // Synthesis

Arisa abandons the AIRIS persona. IAMAI accepts its machine identity. Human and archive continue together as equals.

Production Notes

Risks + Roadmap

Prototype

  • One exploration map
  • Scanner system
  • AIRIS dialogue interaction
  • Country guess interface

Vertical Slice

  • Three normal locations
  • Reality fracture transition
  • Hospital Floor 1
  • Inventory replacement for scanner

Main Risks

  • Exploration may become repetitive
  • Genre shift may feel disconnected
  • AIRIS dialogue must feel mysterious, not confusing
  • Hospital horror should not erase the geography identity

Testing Questions

  • Is scanning and guessing fun enough?
  • Do players want harder countries?
  • Do players prefer story, horror, or both?
  • Should chase sequences exist?
